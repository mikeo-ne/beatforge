What this thing actually does

Step sequencer → MIDI

Program a beat on the grid, hear it instantly (Web Audio synthesis, no samples needed), then export a Standard MIDI File. Drum lanes are written to MIDI channel 10 with General MIDI note numbers (kick 36, snare 38, clap 39, closed hat 42, open hat 46), so dragging the file onto a Drum Kit Designer track in Logic Pro lines up first time. Pitched lanes go to channels 1–3.

Your own one-shots

Open Kit — your own one-shots below the tracks. Drop a sound on a lane or tap Choose sound on a phone/tablet. The sample is additive and local: it replaces that lane's synth voice without changing the pattern. Use trim, gain, tune, pitch-follow, one-shot/gated, reverse and timing shift; clearing it returns the synth.

Humanize

Humanize is a repeatable performance layer, not random jitter. Its integer hash keeps every loop identical and timing is limited to 45% of one step. Turn on Write feel into MIDI to preserve timing, velocity and length when you export to a DAW.

Genres

18 styles in four families, each written as an authentic signature groove rather than a generic loop — house is four-on-the-floor with offbeat bass and open hats, reggaeton is the dembow kick on 1 & 3 with the snare on 3/6/11/14, amapiano is a log-drum bass sliding through the scale under a 16th shaker, drum & bass is a 2-step break with ghost snares. Loading a genre sets its tempo, length, swing and pattern together.

Patterns are verified in the test suite: tests/genre-rhythms.cjs asserts each genre's signature rhythm (22 checks — four-on-the-floor, dembow placement, half-time snare, ghost notes, triplet kicks, swung 8ths), and tests/genre-export.cjs loads all 18, range-checks every step and pitch, and builds a valid MIDI file for each.

Randomise is genre-aware: it keeps the loaded style's rhythmic feel, then drops a few hits, adds fill hits, and moves pitches within the genre's scale (each genre declares its root and mode — minor, dorian, major, phrygian).

Rendering your pattern to audio

Render WAV bounces the pattern with OfflineAudioContext — faster than real time, in the browser, through the same synth voices and master chain you hear. Output is 44.1 kHz 16-bit stereo PCM, peak-normalised to −0.4 dBFS, with a 2 s tail so the reverb isn't cut off. Length is ×1/×2/×4/×8 loops of whatever you programmed. Logic Pro, Live, FL and Reaper all import it directly.

MP3 uses the WebCodecs AudioEncoder, which Chrome and Edge ship but Safari does not always expose. If it isn't available the button tells you and points at WAV rather than failing quietly — so WAV is the path that always works.

Audio → MIDI

The server analyses the waveform: a per-band whitened spectral-flux onset detector finds the hits, each hit is classified by which band its attack lives in (sub → kick, mid → clap/snare, high → hat), and a YIN pitch tracker with loudness weighting transcribes the bass and melody lines. Tempo comes from a comb-filtered autocorrelation, then the period is refined against the onset grid — without that last step a 0.1% tempo error drifts a whole 16th note after 20 bars.

Two engines, same idea

BEATFORGE ships two transcriber implementations so it never depends on a server:

Server engine (server.py, numpy/scipy) — bigger FFTs, fully band-limited pitch tracking, the most accurate. Used automatically when the server is reachable, or when you pick “Server only”.
In-browser engine — a JavaScript port that runs on decoded audio with no backend at all. Works from a downloaded file, offline, in a sandboxed preview, anywhere. Faster, and drum-accurate, but its pitched lanes are noticeably weaker.

Measured accuracy

Drum lanes scored against a known 140 BPM arrangement — 42 s of audio, bars 1–24, including every 16th-note hat run and 32nd roll in it (bench.py):

100% / 100%kick recall / precision
92% / 96%clap recall / precision
70% / 97%closed hat recall / precision
±0%tempo error
59%bass line recall (server)
40–58%melody recall

The drum lanes went through three rounds of measurement. Two findings mattered: spectral flux is scale-invariant, so low-frequency ripple in near-silence registers as much "flux" as a real kick (a false kick at 7.18 s had 4.8 flux but only 12% of a real kick's energy — fixed with an attack test on absolute band energy); and a kick's own pitch sweep (150 → 46 Hz) crosses several FFT bins and invents extra onsets, so per-band picking is only safe for the high band. Hats are the remaining weak lane: the model is strongest on the loud backbeats and weaker on quiet 16th runs, and precision is high — extras are usually real hits the detector places one grid step away, not noise.

Bass and melody still want the server engine, and still want stems over full mixes: single-channel transcription cannot fully separate a melody from a sustaining pad underneath it.
